Optimising General Configuration of Wing-Sailed Autonomous Sailing Monohulls Using Bayesian Optimisation and Knowledge Transfer

Yang An*, Feng Hu, Kuo Chen, and Jiancheng Yu*. (2023). "Optimising General Configuration of Wing-Sailed Autonomous Sailing Monohulls Using Bayesian Optimisation and Knowledge Transfer." Journal of Marine Science and Engineering. 11(4), 703.

Download PDF DOI / Publisher

摘要 / Abstract

翼帆无人单体帆船通常依据任务载荷与目标海域进行定制设计，其总体构型（帆、船体与龙骨之间的尺寸匹配）需在安全性与航行效率之间取得权衡。由于设计空间广阔，且难以从既有方案获得可靠的性能先验估计，传统基于离线代理模型的仿真驱动设计往往需要大量计算流体力学（CFD）采样，计算代价较高。

本文提出一种融合贝叶斯优化（BO）与知识迁移（KT）的通用仿真驱动框架：BO 将采样自适应地集中于关键工况附近，KT 代理模型则复用历史 CFD 信息，从而在缺乏初始设计与先验估计的条件下仍可实现高质量、低成本的总体构型优化。在“Seagull”原型的设计约束下（例如横倾角不超过 26°），优化方案使迎风航速提高约 56%、顺风航速提高约 106%；与离线方法相比，仿真成本可降低约 40%–60%。该框架为任务定制型无人帆船提供了可推广的总体设计方法，并有望拓展至水翼耦合等更为复杂的系统。

Wing-sailed autonomous sailing monohulls are commonly designed for specific payloads and target sea areas. Their general configuration—namely, the size matching among sail, hull, and keel—requires a careful trade-off between safety and sailing efficiency. The associated design space is large, and reliable prior performance estimates are difficult to obtain from historical designs; consequently, conventional offline surrogate-based simulation-driven design typically demands extensive computational fluid dynamics (CFD) sampling at high computational cost.

This paper proposes a generalised simulation-driven framework that integrates Bayesian optimisation (BO) with knowledge transfer (KT). BO adaptively concentrates sampling near critical operating conditions, while KT surrogates reuse historical CFD information, enabling high-quality and computationally efficient configuration optimisation even in the absence of an initial design and prior estimates. Under the design constraints of the “Seagull” prototype (for example, a heel angle not exceeding 26°), the optimised configuration improves upwind speed by approximately 56% and downwind speed by approximately 106%; relative to offline methods, simulation cost can be reduced by approximately 40%–60%. The framework provides a transferable methodology for mission-specific autonomous sailboat design and may be extended to more complex systems involving hydrofoil coupling.
